Large Language Model (LLM)

A Large Language Model is a type of AI model trained on massive amounts of text data that can understand, generate, and reason about human language.

What is an LLM?

An LLM is a neural network (specifically, a Transformer) with billions of parameters, trained by reading enormous amounts of text from books, websites, code, and other sources. Through this training, it learns the patterns and structure of language — grammar, facts, reasoning, and even some common sense.

Examples of LLMs include Claude (Anthropic), GPT-4 (OpenAI), Gemini (Google), and Llama (Meta). These models power chatbots, writing assistants, code generators, and AI agents.

How LLMs Work

At their core, LLMs predict the next token (word or word-piece) in a sequence. Given "The cat sat on the ___", the model predicts "mat" is likely. By chaining these predictions together and being trained on trillions of tokens, LLMs can generate coherent paragraphs, solve math problems, write code, and carry on conversations.

Key Characteristics

  • Scale: Billions of parameters (Claude Opus has hundreds of billions)
  • Context window: How much text they can process at once
  • Training data: Trained on internet-scale text corpora
  • Emergent abilities: Larger models develop capabilities not present in smaller ones

Layman's Analogy

An LLM is like someone who has read every book in every library in the world and can now have a conversation about any topic. They don't "know" things the way humans do — they've learned patterns about how words relate to each other. But the result looks remarkably like understanding.
