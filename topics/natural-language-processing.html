Natural Language Processing (NLP)

Natural Language Processing is the branch of AI focused on enabling computers to understand, interpret, and generate human language — the technology that lets you talk to machines in plain English.

What is NLP?

Human language is messy, ambiguous, and context-dependent. "Bank" can mean a river bank or a financial bank. "I saw her duck" has two completely different meanings. NLP is the field that tackles these challenges — building systems that can read, understand, and produce natural language.

Large language models like Claude represent the current state of the art in NLP — they can understand context, nuance, and even humor in ways that were impossible a few years ago.

NLP Tasks

  • Text generation: Writing essays, code, emails (what LLMs do)
  • Sentiment analysis: Determining if text is positive/negative
  • Translation: Converting between languages
  • Summarization: Condensing long documents
  • Named entity recognition: Identifying names, places, dates in text
  • Question answering: Finding answers in documents

Layman's Analogy

NLP is like teaching a computer to be fluent in a human language. Before NLP, talking to a computer was like talking to someone who only understands exact commands ("ERROR: command not found"). With NLP, it's like talking to a fluent speaker who understands slang, context, and even sarcasm.
