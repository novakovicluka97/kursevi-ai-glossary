Structured Data (JSON-LD / Schema.org)

Structured data is machine-readable code (in JSON-LD format) embedded in your web pages that explicitly tells search engines and AI systems what your content is — not just what it says, but what it means.

What is Structured Data?

When Google reads your page, it sees text. It can guess that "Marie Curie" is a person and "1867" is a year, but it might be wrong. Structured data removes the guessing — you explicitly declare: "This page is about a Person. Her name is Marie Curie. She was born in 1867. She won 2 Nobel Prizes."

The vocabulary comes from Schema.org (a standard maintained by Google, Microsoft, Yahoo, and Yandex). The format is JSON-LD — a block of JSON inside a <script> tag that doesn't affect the visible page.

What It Looks Like

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Article",
  "headline": "What is JSON?",
  "description": "JSON is a lightweight data format for AI APIs.",
  "author": {
    "@type": "Person",
    "name": "Your Name"
  },
  "datePublished": "2026-05-16",
  "publisher": {
    "@type": "Organization",
    "name": "AI Glossary"
  }
}
</script>

Why It Matters in 2026

Structured data has become dramatically more important because of AI search:

  • 35% higher click-through rates — Pages with structured data earn "rich results" (star ratings, FAQ dropdowns, images) in Google that stand out visually
  • 2.3x more likely to appear in AI Overviews — Google's AI answers and Perplexity cite structured pages more often
  • AI systems parse it reliably — LLMs can extract JSON-LD data more accurately than unstructured prose
  • Knowledge Graph recognition — Google's entity database uses structured data to understand who and what you are

Most Important Schema Types

  1. Organization — Who you are as a company/entity
  2. Article / BlogPosting — Content pages (like this glossary)
  3. FAQPage — Question-and-answer content
  4. Product — E-commerce product pages
  5. LocalBusiness — Physical business locations

Why JSON-LD?

Google recommends JSON-LD over alternatives (Microdata, RDFa) because it lives in a separate <script> block, doesn't mix with your HTML, is easier to maintain, and is easier for AI crawlers to parse.

Layman's Analogy

Regular HTML is like writing a résumé as a free-form essay. Structured data is like filling out a standardized form: Name ___, Job Title ___, Years of Experience ___. Both contain the same information, but the form lets a computer instantly extract and compare every field. That's what structured data does for search engines — turns your prose into a standardized form they can read instantly.
