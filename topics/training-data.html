Training Data

Training data is the collection of examples that an AI model learns from during the machine learning process — the "textbooks" the model studies.

What is Training Data?

Every AI model is shaped by its training data. For large language models, training data includes books, websites, academic papers, code repositories, and other text sources — often trillions of tokens worth. The model learns language patterns, facts, reasoning, and style from this data.

The quality, diversity, and size of training data directly determines the model's capabilities and limitations. A model trained only on English text won't understand French. A model never shown code won't know how to program.

Key Concepts

  • Knowledge cutoff: The model only knows what was in its training data. Claude's knowledge has a cutoff date after which it doesn't know about events.
  • Bias: If training data over-represents certain viewpoints, the model will too.
  • Data quality: "Garbage in, garbage out" — poor quality data leads to poor model performance.
  • Scale: Modern LLMs are trained on multiple terabytes of text data.

Layman's Analogy

Training data is to AI what life experience is to a person. A person raised reading only science fiction would have a very different worldview than someone who read everything. Similarly, an AI model's "personality" and knowledge come entirely from what it was trained on. The better and more diverse the training data, the more capable and balanced the model.
