Inference

Inference is when a trained AI model generates output — the moment it actually produces a response to your prompt. It's the "using" phase as opposed to the "learning" phase.

What is Inference?

An AI model's life has two phases: training (learning from data, which takes weeks/months and costs millions) and inference (using what it learned to generate responses, which happens in seconds). Every time you chat with Claude, you're running inference.

How Inference Works for LLMs

During inference, an LLM generates text one token at a time. For each token, it:

  1. Looks at all previous tokens (the context)
  2. Calculates probabilities for every possible next token
  3. Selects one token (influenced by temperature)
  4. Adds that token to the context and repeats

This is why AI responses appear word by word — it's literally generating one token at a time.

Inference Costs

Running inference requires powerful hardware (GPUs/TPUs). AI APIs charge per token of inference — both input tokens (your prompt) and output tokens (the response). Faster, cheaper inference is a major area of research and competition.

Layman's Analogy

Training is like going to school for 16 years. Inference is like using that education at your job every day. School is expensive and takes a long time, but once you've graduated, you can quickly apply what you learned to new problems. Similarly, training a model costs millions, but each inference (each response) is cheap and fast.
