Token

A token is the basic unit of text that an LLM processes — roughly equivalent to a word or word-piece. It's how AI "reads" and "writes" language.

What is a Token?

Large language models don't read individual letters or whole sentences — they break text into tokens. A token is typically about 3-4 characters or roughly ¾ of a word. Common words like "the" are one token; longer or rarer words get split into multiple tokens. For example, "understanding" might be split into "under" + "standing."

Why Tokens Matter

  • Cost: AI APIs charge per token processed. More tokens = more expensive.
  • Context window: Models have a maximum number of tokens they can handle at once (e.g., 200K tokens for Claude).
  • Speed: Models generate one token at a time. More tokens = slower response.

Token Counts

As a rough guide: 1 token ≈ 4 characters in English. So 100 tokens ≈ 75 words. A full page of text is about 300-400 tokens. A novel is about 100,000 tokens. Claude's context window of 200K tokens means it can process roughly the equivalent of two full novels at once.

Layman's Analogy

Tokens are like puzzle pieces that make up language. Instead of reading letter by letter (too slow) or sentence by sentence (too big), the AI reads in word-sized chunks. It's like how you read — you don't look at each letter in "cat," you see the whole word as one unit.
