Hallucination

A hallucination is when an AI model generates information that sounds confident and plausible but is factually incorrect, fabricated, or nonsensical.

What is an AI Hallucination?

LLMs don't "know" things the way humans do — they predict likely token sequences based on patterns in their training data. Sometimes these patterns produce text that reads well but is simply wrong: fake citations, invented facts, non-existent APIs, or historical events that never happened.

Hallucinations are a fundamental challenge in AI because the model expresses fabricated information with the same confidence as accurate information — making it hard for users to tell the difference without verification.

Common Examples

  • Citing academic papers that don't exist
  • Inventing plausible-sounding statistics
  • Describing features of software that were never built
  • Attributing quotes to people who never said them

Mitigation Strategies

  • RAG: Ground responses in retrieved sources
  • Citation requirements: Ask the model to cite sources (makes fabrication more obvious)
  • Low temperature: Reduce randomness for factual tasks
  • Human verification: Always fact-check critical information
  • Safety training: Models like Claude are trained to say "I don't know" rather than guess

Layman's Analogy

A hallucination is like a student who didn't study for the exam but writes confidently anyway — filling in plausible-sounding answers that happen to be completely wrong. The essay reads well, the logic seems sound, but the facts are made up. That's what AI does when it hallucinates.
