Meta Tags & OG Tags

Meta tags are invisible HTML elements in your page's <head> that tell search engines and social platforms what your page is about, how to display it, and how to index it.

What Are Meta Tags?

Meta tags don't appear on the visible page — they live in the HTML source code. Search engines read them to understand your content, and social platforms read them to generate link previews. The two most important for SEO:

  • Title tag (<title>): The clickable headline in Google search results. Keep it under 60 characters. This is the single most important on-page SEO element.
  • Meta description (<meta name="description">): The 1-2 sentence summary shown below the title in search results. Keep it under 160 characters. Doesn't directly affect ranking, but directly affects whether people click.

Essential Meta Tags

<head>
  <!-- Page title (shows in browser tab + search results) -->
  <title>What is JSON? - AI Glossary</title>

  <!-- Description (shows below title in search results) -->
  <meta name="description" content="JSON is a lightweight data format
    used for AI APIs, structured outputs, and automation configs.">

  <!-- Character encoding (always UTF-8) -->
  <meta charset="UTF-8">

  <!-- Mobile responsive (required for mobile search ranking) -->
  <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1.0">

  <!-- Canonical URL (prevents duplicate content issues) -->
  <link rel="canonical" href="https://yoursite.com/topics/json.html">
</head>

Open Graph (OG) Tags

OG tags control how your page looks when shared on social media (LinkedIn, Twitter/X, Slack, Discord, Facebook). Without them, platforms show a generic link. With them, you get a rich preview with image, title, and description. 

<!-- Open Graph tags (social sharing) -->
<meta property="og:title" content="What is JSON? - AI Glossary">
<meta property="og:description" content="JSON explained for AI practitioners.">
<meta property="og:image" content="https://yoursite.com/images/json-preview.png">
<meta property="og:url" content="https://yoursite.com/topics/json.html">
<meta property="og:type" content="article">

<!-- Twitter/X card -->
<meta name="twitter:card" content="summary_large_image">
<meta name="twitter:title" content="What is JSON? - AI Glossary">
<meta name="twitter:description" content="JSON explained for AI practitioners.">
<meta name="twitter:image" content="https://yoursite.com/images/json-preview.png">

Impact

Your title tag and meta description are what people see before they decide to click on your page. A compelling title with a clear description can double your click-through rate from search results — even without improving your ranking position. OG tags determine whether your content looks professional or generic when shared in Slack, LinkedIn, or on social media.

Layman's Analogy

Meta tags are like the cover and back-cover blurb of a book. The title tag is the book title on the spine — what people see when scanning the shelf (search results). The meta description is the blurb — what convinces them to pick it up. OG tags are the book jacket design — what makes it look good when someone holds it up to show a friend (shares a link).
